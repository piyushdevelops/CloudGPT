# June Shop homepage audit — 28 September 2026

Read-only review of unpublished theme 187527266499. No Shopify files, settings or publication state changed. Section 1 remains locked; findings there are recommendations for a separately authorised revision.

## Scope and evidence

Fresh remote sources and homepage configuration were captured in `remote-snapshot.json`; current section source copies are in `source/`. Reviewed the current preview at 1440 × 1000 and 390 × 844, plus a narrow 320 × 740 document-overflow check. Desktop and mobile evidence is saved as `desktop-01.png` through `desktop-12.png` and `mobile-01.png` through `mobile-12.png`. Additional comparison and Mystery Box disclosure screenshots are included. Some screenshots include neighbouring sections; they are viewport captures, not cropped section exports.

Reviewed the project handover, locked typography, section implementation records and approved reference boards. The reference contact sheet is `reference-contact-sheet.jpg`. Later user decisions take precedence over the boards: Poppins, peach merchant accent, real catalog products and the revised Section 3 reveal are not errors merely because they differ from older illustrative boards.

Tested representative hero states, planner comparison, Limited Edition reveal and cover changes, interior viewer/Escape, baby chapter selection, and Mystery Box disclosure. Inspected current markup, scripts and settings. This is not an exhaustive accessibility certification, checkout test, device-lab test or measured performance audit. Newsletter forms were not submitted, and email delivery was not tested. Current product availability should be rechecked before launch; historical stock counts are not current evidence.

**Labels:** FIX = observed behaviour or source discrepancy; IMPROVE = design/usability recommendation; VERIFY = unresolved operational check. P1 = address before launch or section sign-off; P2 = meaningful usability/conversion improvement; P3 = polish. These are priorities, not claims that every item is a technical defect.

## Overall assessment

The page has a coherent identity and several effective compositions. The biggest opportunity is not more decoration: it is making each section earn its height and answer a different shopping question. The desktop page is approximately 9,665 pixels tall. Mobile measurements total approximately 12,260 pixels across the 12 sections in the tested states, excluding the header; the Limited Edition reveal was open and the chapter section was pinned. Heights vary with selection and viewport.

The first six sections consume about 59% of desktop section height, broadly consistent with the planner-led business brief. Preserve that emphasis while reducing unnecessary vertical travel. In particular, Sections 2, 3, 6 and 10 need attention on mobile.

### Page-wide and header improvements

1. **P1 — IMPROVE: shorten the path to a product.** Reduce stage heights and excessive separation between selection, price and CTA. Do not simply shrink every font. Mobile Section 2 is about 1,242px, open Section 3 about 1,453px, Section 6 about 1,801px including scroll travel, and Section 10 about 1,182px.
2. **P2 — IMPROVE: vary informational purpose.** Sections 2/3/5/6/7/8/9/10 repeatedly ask users to choose something and read an updated panel. Keep useful interactions, but give each a distinct purpose: compare, inspect, demonstrate, shop a scene, understand light, choose capacity, build a setting.
3. **P2 — IMPROVE: loosen tight heading tracking selectively.** Several headings visually merge words. Keep the locked Poppins family, weight direction and natural glyph width; use the existing per-section spacing and size controls to improve word separation. Do not restore old condensed reference typography or apply horizontal scaling.
4. **P2 — VERIFY: test sticky header offsets with real anchor navigation.** The floating mobile search header occupies the top strip during scroll. Check skip links, tab-driven jumps and section links against its actual expanded/collapsed height. Screenshots captured beneath it alone do not prove a clipping bug.
5. **P2 — IMPROVE: simplify the animated search prompt.** The current typing text is frequently partial, and longer suggestions truncate. Prefer a stable, useful placeholder or shorter suggestions. Retain a persistent accessible input label independent of the animation.
6. **P1 — VERIFY: complete launch QA separately.** Test signup delivery/segmentation, actual payment methods, product/cart templates with the new global footer, keyboard and screen-reader journeys, reduced-motion behaviour on a real setting, and measured mobile loading performance. No console errors were returned in the sampled browser log, and no document-wide horizontal overflow appeared at 390px or 320px; neither proves comprehensive correctness.

## 1 — Make Room hero (locked)

2. **P1 — VERIFY: reconcile the Baby Record Books hero link with Section 6.** The Life Journals tab offers a normal shopping link while Section 6 explicitly says the books are unavailable. Recheck the collection, then use consistent availability messaging. An unavailable notice is confirmed in Section 6; the audit does not assert today's live collection is empty.
3. **P2 — IMPROVE: bring a mobile shopping action into the first view.** The default hero measures about 981px; at 390 × 844 the initial viewport shows the large product image while the CTA begins below the visible bottom. Reduce mobile image/stage height or place one clear action above the image, preserving desktop gutters.
4. **P2 — IMPROVE: expose category navigation sooner on mobile.** Tabs and Mystery Box access follow the long hero. A more compact mobile composition would make the range discoverable before nearly a screen of scrolling.

6. **P2 — IMPROVE: give the 2027 tab a distinct role from Section 4.** The same launch signup appears twice. Keep the hero version compact and use Section 4 for meaningful launch context; make the email promise consistent in both places.

## 2 — Undated planners

1. **P1 — IMPROVE: make the comparison actually compare.** The tested modal presents cover, name, price and availability, but no meaningful differences. Add verified size, page count, layout and included features. If interiors are identical, say so plainly and explain which differences remain.
2. **P2 — IMPROVE: compress the section.** At about 1,079px desktop and 1,242px mobile, cover selection and the purchase strip are far apart. Reduce the cover stage and interior preview footprint while keeping a clear selected planner.
3. **P2 — IMPROVE: connect the selected cover to buying details more closely.** On mobile the name is near the cover, while price and CTA follow the large interior panel. Keep name, price and action together or repeat a compact selected-product summary near the action. **The current price is visible on mobile; it is not missing.**
4. **P2 — IMPROVE: make the inside-page image inspectable.** The layout image contains small writing and annotations. Reuse the deliberate viewer pattern from Section 5, or provide a useful detail crop instead of an unzoomable preview.
5. **P2 — IMPROVE: explain selection with less duplication.** Large covers, thumbnails, a numeric counter and a name all repeat the same state on mobile. Keep the peeking carousel plus clear thumbnails/name, and reduce redundant visual indicators.
6. **P2 — VERIFY: add/check a complete no-JavaScript browsing fallback.** Source relies on button-driven selection and has no dedicated no-script list for all three products. Ensure every featured product remains reachable when enhancement fails, without requiring the comparison dialog or cover-switching script.

## 3 — Limited Edition / With extras

1. **P1 — IMPROVE: replace ambiguous gift imagery.** The personalised alphabet-keychain gift is illustrated with a “Hey Girl” style photo rather than a clear first-letter example. Use an accurate alphabet example and keep the shipping-name explanation immediately beside it.
2. **P2 — IMPROVE: shorten the reveal-to-purchase journey.** The open mobile state measured about 1,453px. Keep cover selection, price and CTA near the planner; let the extras expand beneath that compact purchase group.
3. **P2 — IMPROVE: make the entire gift set discoverable.** Mobile gift cards extend horizontally. Add a visible count/progress cue and an obvious next-card affordance or compact text summary so shoppers do not miss the remaining inclusions.
4. **P2 — IMPROVE: make the closed state sell the offer.** Large empty space and a single planner create a weaker initial explanation than a compact grouped package view. Show an accurate hint of the included set before “unpacking,” without returning to a generic card grid.
5. **P2 — IMPROVE: add visible cover names.** Small cover thumbnails are hard to identify by artwork alone. Show a short name on focus/selection and make the current cover/count relationship obvious.
6. **P1 — VERIFY: reconcile gift names, photos and counts against fulfilment before launch.** The implementation correctly changes the displayed count between five and three in the sampled states. Preserve that per-product logic. Confirm the three-gift cover's text-only socks treatment and composite notebook photo, rather than filling gaps with invented gift imagery or a universal “five gifts” claim.

## 4 — 2027 Next Chapter

1. **P1 — VERIFY: complete the launch-email workflow.** A working native signup form is not proof that the promised launch email will be sent. Verify the customer tag/segment, consent handling, campaign ownership and delivery using an authorised test address.
2. **P1 — IMPROVE: align the signup promise.** “An email when our 2027 planners launch” is specific; “Sign up for email updates” is broader. Use accurate, consistent wording here and in the hero, based on the actual subscription behaviour.
3. **P2 — IMPROVE: remove repetitive coming-soon copy.** Next year, 2027, coming soon, plans in the making, launch list and first look compete to make one point. Keep the strong date-card idea and one benefit-led signup message.
4. **P2 — IMPROVE: keep this a compact interlude on mobile.** The section is about 683px. Tighten the gap between the date card and signup ticket and reduce decorative vertical padding.
5. **P2 — IMPROVE: add one useful reason to join.** Use an approved launch window or a verified detail about the forthcoming planners. Do not invent dates, early access or discounts simply to improve the copy.
6. **P1 — VERIFY: review submitted and error states.** Check invalid email, already-subscribed, successful signup and server error states, including keyboard focus and screen-reader announcement. No forms were submitted during this read-only audit.

## 5 — Inside Discovery

1. **P1 — IMPROVE: align the pictured product and destination.** The displayed interiors are identified in a small caption as Dolce Far Niente Limited Edition, while the main link says “Explore undated planners.” Make the depicted range clear beside the action and provide a direct route to that planner when appropriate.
2. **P2 — IMPROVE: use clean interior photography.** Embedded marketing annotations and props reduce the usable page area. Show a clean spread with sharp fields and natural detail, retaining the catalog image only where its annotations add value.
3. **P2 — IMPROVE: make each tab teach something specific.** Goals, Weekly plans, Habits and Reflection need one concrete explanation of how the page is used, not only motivational copy.
4. **P2 — IMPROVE: improve detail inspection.** The viewer uses the displayed image source; the inspected image was 1080px wide. Use a genuinely higher-resolution source if available, and provide a clear zoom/pan experience for reading small fields.
5. **P2 — IMPROVE: make viewer instructions conditional.** “Scroll across the page” is shown even when the desktop image fits. Show this instruction only when overflow exists, and clarify mobile panning without implying that all screens need it.
6. **P2 — IMPROVE: add a real physical demonstration when assets are available.** A short genuine page-flip or writing clip would better fulfil the approved tactile direction than another static selector. Keep it user-initiated and retain the current image fallback; no video was supplied for the existing build.

## 6 — Other Chapters

1. **P1 — IMPROVE: reduce the required scroll travel.** The pinned story accounts for about 1,700px desktop and 1,801px mobile. Shorten chapter distances or use the manual switcher on phones so the section does not feel like a pause in shopping.
2. **P2 — IMPROVE: make “Skip the story” visibly discoverable.** A keyboard-oriented skip link exists, but touch users should also have a clear way past the long sequence. Keep the bypass close to the chapter controls.
3. **P2 — IMPROVE: distinguish pausing effects from pausing chapter changes.** The implementation freezes the story state while retaining scroll space. Clarify the control or let users continue chapter navigation without travelling through an apparently static section.
4. **P1 — IMPROVE: clarify rewritable imagery.** The build record confirms the board and demonstration photos show different cover artwork. Use matching demonstration assets or an explicit representative-layout caption so shoppers understand what they will receive.
5. **P2 — IMPROVE: enlarge the mobile motion control.** The observed Pause motion target is 32px high. Increase its hit area and preserve readable text; it should be as easy to use as the larger chapter controls.
6. **P2 — IMPROVE: add practical category benefits.** Wedding copy could mention verified budgeting/guest planning; rewritable copy could explain actual reuse and included accessories. Keep the baby unavailable note and absence of a shopping button, as specifically requested by the user.

## 7 — Shop the corner / Home décor

1. **P1 — IMPROVE: strengthen the bridge from styled scene to real product.** The generated scene is atmospheric, but the actual product thumbnail is small. Enlarge it or offer an original-photo detail view so material and proportions can be judged.
2. **P2 — IMPROVE: put the image qualification closer to the scene.** “Styled visual. See product photos for details” currently sits below the broad category CTA. Place it with the image or selected item, where it explains the relevant visual.
3. **P2 — IMPROVE: differentiate the three choices by name.** Hotspots and the second selector repeat 01/02/03. Keep hotspots, but use concise names such as Vase, Birds and Frame in the companion selector.
4. **P2 — IMPROVE: give the selected product stronger action priority.** The large filled button promotes all décor while the selected product has a smaller underlined action. Make the current item's shopping route more prominent when a hotspot has been deliberately selected.
5. **P2 — IMPROVE: add a useful specification.** Include verified dimensions/material or quantity alongside price. This is more helpful than another decorative caption and helps people judge how it fits their space.
6. **P2 — IMPROVE: tighten the mobile selection layout.** Heading, scene, product card, repeated numbered selector and category CTA span about 807px. Reduce duplicate controls or bring selection and product details closer together.

## 8 — Find your glow / Lighting

1. **P2 — IMPROVE: make the scene feel intentional at desktop width.** A square catalog photograph sits within a much wider dark stage, leaving a large empty area and visible rectangular photo boundaries. Use a suitable landscape scene or a deliberate framed composition; do not fabricate lamp details.
2. **P2 — IMPROVE: show lamp silhouettes clearly in selectors.** Circular thumbnails contain dark room photography, making the lamps tiny. Use accurate tighter crops with comparable scale and exposure.
3. **P1 — IMPROVE: explain the price differences.** Names and prices alone do not help choose among three similar gold lamps. Add verified dimensions, power method and lighting modes where applicable.
4. **P2 — IMPROVE: substantiate the mood promise.** If the selected product supports multiple colour temperatures, demonstrate genuine modes or describe them clearly. Do not recolour an image to imply unsupported lighting behaviour.
5. **P2 — IMPROVE: shorten the mobile path to price and CTA.** At about 839px, the large image and selector occupy most of the section before the shopping action. Trim stage height and panel padding while retaining useful image detail.
6. **P2 — IMPROVE: improve inactive choice legibility.** Dark, small thumbnails and muted labels make the alternatives look less inviting. Raise their visual clarity while keeping a distinct selected underline and accessible state.

## 9 — Pick your pour / Glassware

1. **P2 — IMPROVE: connect occasions to product types.** Everyday, Slow mornings and Celebrations are evocative but hide that they represent a tumbler, mug and flute set. Add short type labels or small product previews to the selector.
2. **P2 — IMPROVE: use a more purposeful image crop.** On mobile, the glass occupies a modest portion of the square scene. Enlarge the product while preserving rim, base and useful scale context.
3. **P2 — IMPROVE: bring purchase details nearer the selector.** The mobile section measures about 959px, with the product title beginning near the bottom of the first section viewport. Reduce image height and gaps so capacity, quantity, price and CTA are easier to see together.
4. **P2 — IMPROVE: add verified care information.** A short relevant note about cleaning or hot-drink suitability can resolve a real buying question. Verify it per product; do not infer dishwasher safety from appearance.
5. **P2 — IMPROVE: retain quantity distinctions prominently through every state.** The default correctly says “300 ml · Single glass.” Keep equally clear set-of-two and mug-accessory information close to price in the other states so styled photos cannot imply the wrong contents.
6. **P3 — IMPROVE: add an authentic use detail when available.** A genuine pour shot, rim close-up or handle view would provide more information than the current broad lifestyle image. Preserve the user-selected headline; reverting to the board's old heading is not necessary.

## 10 — Layer the table / Kitchen & Dining

1. **P1 — IMPROVE: clearly state that the pieces are sold separately.** The floating three-piece composition can read as a set. The selected detail says “Single ceramic plate,” but add a concise sold-separately note beside the arrangement and price.
2. **P1 — IMPROVE: pair illustrative cutouts with original product imagery.** The build record identifies these as generated adaptations. Offer a selected-item original photo or detail view and clearly distinguish styling from exact colour/shape evidence.
3. **P2 — IMPROVE: reduce mobile height.** At about 1,182px, the large stack and arrangement switch push categories and purchase details far down. Make the arrangement more compact and move the selected-item details up.
4. **P2 — IMPROVE: visibly connect selection to the pictured piece.** The right-hand list highlights Side plates, but the stack itself does not make the selected piece equally obvious. Use a restrained outline, spotlight or corresponding label without obscuring the product.

6. **P2 — IMPROVE: explain what stacking adds.** The Stack/Unstack control is playful but can consume attention without helping selection. Tie it to understanding proportions or keep it visually secondary to choosing a piece and shopping it.

## 11 — Mystery Box

1. **P1 — IMPROVE: define the value guarantee.** The disclosure says “The value is guaranteed” without a number. Display the verified current tier/value relationship, ideally from maintained product data, rather than relying on a vague assurance. Build-time records mention two tiers; recheck before using their amounts.
2. **P2 — IMPROVE: rename “Take a peek” or improve its payoff.** The tested action reveals explanatory text and says the actual reveal happens on delivery. “How the surprise works” would match that behaviour more closely, or use a playful reveal of verified value/category information without inventing contents.
3. **P2 — IMPROVE: explain options beside the starting price.** “From Rs. 1,499” and “See available options” require another click to understand the range. Add a concise description of the available box choices after verifying current variants.
4. **P2 — IMPROVE: avoid duplicated generic surprise copy.** Curiosity, surprise, happy surprise and available details occupy three parts of the same section. Let each part do different work: intrigue, value, then purchase choice.
5. **P2 — IMPROVE: keep important buying conditions outside the optional peek.** “Contents vary and cannot be customised” is useful before shopping. Include a short persistent version near the CTA while keeping the full explanation in the disclosure.
6. **P2 — IMPROVE: use an in-flow mobile disclosure if copy grows.** The current overlay fits the tested content, but covers the artwork and depends on its available area. An expanding panel would be more resilient to longer merchant text and larger text settings. This is a resilience recommendation, not a claim of current clipping.

## 12 — Footer

1. **P1 — VERIFY: reconcile payment icons with actual checkout.** The preview renders only a PayPal icon. Confirm whether this accurately represents the store's active payment methods, including any separate checkout integration. Do not restore unsupported payment logos merely to fill space.
2. **P1 — VERIFY: test the global footer outside the homepage.** The footer-group change affects other templates. Check product, collection, cart and policy pages for navigation, spacing, language/country behaviour and duplicate content before sign-off.
3. **P2 — IMPROVE: explain the newsletter benefit before expansion.** “Email updates” is easy to overlook. Use a clear, truthful benefit and make the privacy explanation accessible near the form. Keep this general newsletter distinct from the 2027 launch list.
4. **P2 — IMPROVE: simplify country selection.** The country selector plus separate Update button feels administrative. Use a clearer label and improve the interaction while preserving an accessible submit mechanism. Confirm that displayed countries match supported service destinations.
5. **P2 — IMPROVE: make support access more immediately useful.** If approved support hours or a direct contact number are available, expose them concisely. Current links are a good foundation; avoid inventing response-time promises.
6. **P3 — IMPROVE: balance the brand wordmark and utility row.** The large wordmark dominates while payment/localisation utilities feel detached at the edge. Adjust spacing and natural font sizing to make the bottom area more deliberate without horizontally scaling the lettering or sacrificing the mobile accordion structure.

## Recommended first implementation pass

1. Resolve factual/operational clarity: gifts, representative imagery, Mystery Box value, sold-separately messaging, email delivery and payment display.
2. Tighten mobile Sections 2, 3, 6 and 10; bring selected product, price and CTA together.
3. Make comparison and product descriptions genuinely useful, particularly Sections 2 and 8.
4. Improve source photos/crops and viewer detail before adding more animation.
5. Review typography spacing and final consistency, retaining the locked design system and Section 1 approval boundary.

### What should be retained

The consistent Poppins/capsule system; desktop centred gutters; deliberate, non-autoplay product selection; accurate dynamic prices; the observed per-cover Limited Edition counts; the requested baby unavailable state without a shopping button; native comparison/viewer Escape behaviour; and the mobile footer's clear accordion structure. The audit is not a recommendation to rebuild all 12 sections.
